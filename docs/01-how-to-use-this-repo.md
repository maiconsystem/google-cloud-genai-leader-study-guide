# How to use this repo

Eight steps. Roughly 40 hours if you are new to gen AI, roughly 12 if you already work with it.

The principle behind the order: **diagnose early, then spend your remaining time only where you are losing marks.** Most people do it backwards — they watch every video, then take one practice exam the night before.

---

## Step 1 — Understand what you are sitting (30 minutes)

Read **[02-exam-facts.md](02-exam-facts.md)**, then skim **[the official exam guide](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/generative_ai_leader_exam_guide_english.pdf)**.

The guide is a list of every examinable topic in Google's own words. Skimming it now tells you how *shallow* the exam is — it is a foundational, business-level certification — which stops you over-preparing on theory and under-preparing on product names.

Then read **[04-product-naming-2026.md](04-product-naming-2026.md)**. Ten minutes, and it prevents the single most common way to study the wrong thing.

---

## Step 2 — Work one course (5–10 hours)

See **[../courses/](../courses/)** for a summary of each course and which to pick.

**If you do only one: [Google Skills path 1951](https://www.skills.google/paths/1951).** Free, official, and written in the current product vocabulary.

Do not aim for completion. Aim to come out able to say what **Agent Search**, **Agent Studio**, **Gemini Enterprise**, **Gemini Notebook**, **Agent Assist** and **Model Garden** each do, in one sentence each.

---

## Step 3 — Read the main study guide (4–6 hours)

**[../study-guide/01-full-study-guide.md](../study-guide/01-full-study-guide.md)**, in order, one domain at a time. It is organised by exam domain, with the low-yield material already stripped out and worked examples throughout.

Read Section 1 (exam technique) properly rather than skipping to the content. On this exam, technique is worth about as much as knowledge.

---

## Step 4 — Take a full practice exam under real conditions (90 minutes)

**This is the step people skip, and it is the one that saves the most time.**

Real conditions: no notes, no pausing, no looking anything up, 90 minutes on a timer. Then **score it by domain, not just overall.** Four numbers — one per domain — tell you exactly where the rest of your study should go.

Options: [this repo's 60 questions](../practice/01-practice-questions.md), the Pluralsight practice exam if you have access, or Google's sample questions (save those for Step 7 — they are best used as a final check).

---

## Step 5 — Fix the patterns, not just the facts (2 hours)

Read **[../practice/03-common-mistakes.md](../practice/03-common-mistakes.md)** and compare it against your own wrong answers.

The point of that file is that wrong answers cluster. Mine fell into four patterns, and three of them were habits of reading rather than gaps in knowledge:

1. **Under-selecting on multi-select questions** — plural nouns in the stem mean multiple answers.
2. **Learning the wrong lifecycle framework** — the exam uses Dream → Select → Scale → Refine → Expand.
3. **Picking the technical answer over the people/process answer.**
4. **Picking a plausible answer over the *primary* one** — re-read the stem's qualifiers after choosing.

Fixing patterns 1 and 3 costs no studying at all. It is worth roughly 4–6 marks.

---

## Step 6 — Drill Domain 2 until it is automatic (3–5 hours)

Domain 2 is ~35% of the exam and is mostly *"which product for this job?"*. It is pure recall, so drill it like vocabulary.

- **[../study-guide/03-product-table.md](../study-guide/03-product-table.md)** — every product, grouped, with a **reverse lookup** table: trigger phrase → product. Cover one column, recall the other. **Drill the reverse direction hardest** — that is the direction the exam tests.
- **[../practice/02-domain-2-product-drill.md](../practice/02-domain-2-product-drill.md)** — 40 scenario-to-product questions.

Target: naming the product within a couple of seconds of reading the scenario, with no hesitation between the four confusable ones (Gemini Notebook / Gemini Enterprise / Agent Search / Workspace with Gemini).

---

## Step 7 — Google's official sample questions (1 hour)

**[The official sample questions](https://forms.gle/soztS7Q74AXBncATA)** — free, and the closest public thing to the real exam.

Do these **last**, as a final check. The feedback text after each answer contains Google's own one-sentence definition of each product; those phrasings are as close to the exam's voice as you can get. If you score well here, you are ready.

**The gate:** 80%+ overall *and* no domain below 75%. If a domain is below that, go back to Step 5 for that domain only.

---

## Step 8 — Book it, then read one page (90 minutes)

Book as soon as you clear the gate. A booked date is the most effective study tool in this repo.

The night before and the morning of, read **[../study-guide/04-exam-morning-sheet.md](../study-guide/04-exam-morning-sheet.md)** and nothing else. No new material in the last 24 hours — on a recognition-heavy exam, cramming measurably hurts.
