## How to take a practice exam properly

The value is in the diagnosis, not the score.

1. **Real conditions.** No notes, no pausing, no lookups, 90 minutes on a timer, one sitting.
2. **Score it by domain.** Four numbers, one per domain — not one overall percentage. The overall number tells you nothing you can act on.
3. **For every wrong answer, write one line** saying why the right answer is right. That single line is worth more than re-watching the module it came from.
4. **Look for the pattern across your wrong answers**, not just the facts. Mine clustered into four habits of reading. See [03-common-mistakes.md](03-common-mistakes.md).
5. **Then study only what the numbers point at.** Re-reading a domain you scored 93% on is wasted time.

**The readiness gate: 80%+ overall, and no domain below 75%.**

## On question sources

Every question in this folder is **original**, written to match the style, length and distractor patterns of the publicly available official sample questions.

**They are not real exam questions.** Real items are covered by Google's exam agreement and nobody may publish them. You do not need them: what you can legitimately practise is question *style*, and on this exam style is most of the battle.

Where a question is adapted from a course's own published example, it says so.
